Most cells in Pyr are a few sessions of work. The cells in this quest are not. They are giant interneurons in the medial entorhinal cortex (MEC), with so many branches that one cell can take weeks to finish, a little at a time. Nobody has mapped these cell types before, and you can be among the first to see one whole.

## What these cells are

The medial entorhinal cortex helps the brain keep track of where you are. It is home to grid cells, which fire in a regular pattern as an animal moves through space. Interneurons are the cells that hold that activity in check: they inhibit their neighbours, and the giant ones reach a great many of them.

## How a Super Quest works

Open the Cell Library on MEC and pick the **Super Quests** chip. Each row shows the cell's group. Claim one, and it is yours:

- **One at a time, for up to 2 months.** A Super Quest sits beside your other claims and does not count against them.
- **Your time is counted.** Pyr adds up your active time on the cell and shows it on the row, such as 14 h 20 min so far. It counts only while the cell is on screen, Pyr is the tab in front, and you have used the mouse or keys in the last 5 minutes. It adds up across visits and devices. This is how the lab learns how long these cells really take, so there is no need to hurry.
- **Your work is kept.** Save view keeps your layers and marks with the claim, and the claim keeps the cell's ID through every merge and split.
- **Marks.** Mark the AIS, where the axon leaves the cell; it is the one mark that is required. Use **True End** for a branch that stops naturally, and **Checked** for a place you have looked at and found right, so you know where you have been. **Hide marks** clears them from the view when you want to see the cell itself.

Pyr congratulates you at 10, 25, 50 and 100 hours on one cell, and when you finish.

## What to expect

These cells are big, and parts of them leave the data: some branches run past the edge of the imaged volume, and no amount of proofreading will bring those back. That is fine. Finish what is in the data, mark the AIS, and complete the cell. A cell with a few branches cut off at the edge is still a cell that has never been seen whole before.

Thank you for taking one on.
